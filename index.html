What Is a Ham?

A ham is an amateur radio operator: someone who uses radios to talk across town, across the country, or even around the world just for the joy of learning, helping, and making connections.

Think of it as a mashup of science lab, community club, and global adventure powered by antennas, curiosity, and a friendly shout of "CQ!"

Amateur radio highlights

Why “Ham”?

The nickname has been around for generations. Today it simply means a licensed amateur radio operator: a person exploring radio as a hobby, a skill, and a service to others.

What Hams Do

Hams chat on local repeaters, bounce signals off the atmosphere, send digital messages, build antennas, contact the International Space Station, and learn electronics along the way.

Why It Matters

Amateur radio is fun on an ordinary day and useful on a hard day. Operators often support events, public service, and emergency communication when regular systems become unreliable.

How to Start

Study for an entry-level license, visit a local radio club, and get on the air. The hobby welcomes experimenters, tinkerers, makers, and anyone who likes meeting people through technology.

Fun fact: A handheld radio can open the door to a giant hobby. One day you might be chatting with a nearby operator, and the next you could be collecting contacts from all over the map.
